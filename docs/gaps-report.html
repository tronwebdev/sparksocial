The last three, and the judgement each needed

All three were storage and a surface, and in each the interesting part was deciding what the product is allowed to claim. Recorded because those are the decisions worth disagreeing with.

The conversation drawer

ClosedENG-02.4 · §8.8

The judgement: never merge two people. Rows were keyed by the platform's per-message id, so nothing said which belonged to one exchange — a lead that took four messages read as four strangers. When a platform supplies no conversation id, the key is derived, and the derivation always includes the author handle. The cost is that a group thread fragments; the alternative is a stranger's words appearing in somebody else's drawer, and only one of those is a privacy failure.

It also reversed a documented decision. markReplied's comment argued the sent text needed no column because the audit row had it — right for "was this answered?", wrong once the question became "show me the conversation", since tool_calls never returns inputs. A transcript built from the feed alone showed a customer talking to nobody. The comment now says so rather than being quietly deleted.

The influencer watchlist

Closed§8.9

The judgement: refuse by name rather than return nothing. Reading a watched account's posts is a listening capability on the same approval clock as the inbox, so InfluencerSource is a seam with no configured implementation here. trend.influencer.review therefore says it cannot look — because "your competitors posted nothing" and "we cannot see their posts" are different facts and only one is true.

Watching also had to mean something, or this would have been another table nothing consults — the exact shape of gap the rest of this page has been closing. A watched account's posts are scored by the same scoreTrend machinery trend.rank uses, because two scorers for one question drift. And the watchlist is closed to viewer and client, unlike the trend feed: a list of the accounts a brand studies is frequently a list of its competitors.

A/B variants

ClosedDISC-02 · §8.9

The judgement: two posts are not a sample, and the tool has to say so. A verdict from two engagement numbers is a comfortable lie — the bigger number really is bigger. So there are three named ways to have no winner (waiting to publish, waiting for reach, genuinely too close), a 200-impression floor, a deliberately coarse 1.5× threshold that admits it is a heuristic, and "two posts is a hint, not proof" travels in the explanation rather than in a doc comment nobody reading a dashboard will see.

One correction worth recording: the first version computed its own reward and wrote to the learning loop, with a comment explaining why that was fine. It was not. learning.record_outcome scores against the brand's own recent baseline and refuses a caller-supplied number, and a second formula would drift from it — the same "two scorers, one question" mistake the influencer card names. The result tool is now a read that names the winning post; recording is a separate, explicit call.

Not code — approvals, keys, or a decision

Eight items no amount of engineering closes, and the reason each is stuck. This is where the alpha actually lives.

Platform approvals gate publishing, and always did

External§8 · Part A

The real constraint on shipping, restated because it is easy to lose behind a list of code gaps. The PRD’s own integrations register puts LinkedIn at weeks-to-months and says it “rejects anything resembling a generic scheduler”; Meta App Review is one to three weeks; TikTok has a sandbox audit whose posts are invisible until it clears. Fourteen platforms are nameable and every unconfigured one routes to the stub adapter, so calendar → guardrails → policy → publish runs end to end today without a single vendor account. Whether they are reachable is a filing question.

No webhook feeds the engagement inbox

External§8.8

engage.ingest works, is tested, and upserts on (org, genome, platform, external_id) so a webhook retry cannot duplicate a message. Nothing calls it with real traffic, because that needs the Meta listening scopes from the same App Review above. The PRD is right to bundle those scopes into one review cycle rather than two.

WhatsApp Business verification

External§6.2 / §6.3

Needed before a real number can send; the Direct+Finish loop runs end to end on the stub transport until then. Worth restating that this loop deliberately has no web surface — direct.brief.generate, direct.session.send, direct.media.ingest and whatsapp.* are reachable from no screen by design, because the loop is WhatsApp. A brief goes out as a message, the owner films and replies, the reply lands as an asset. A page in the middle would defeat the point.

Corrections found along the way

Recorded rather than quietly edited. A gap tracker that has been wrong once should say where, for the same reason the codebase treats a stale comment as worse than none.

The “one real security hole” was not a hole

The repo’s docs/GAPS.md listed brand-level isolation as an outright gap: team.permission.set writes brand_members rows and supposedly nothing read them, so any org member could reach any brand in the org. Not true. apps/api/src/clerk-auth.ts reads them — every role other than owner/admin needs an explicit row for the claimed brand and is refused with ISOLATION_VIOLATION, absent rather than forbidden, so probing a genome id cannot confirm a brand exists that the caller is not assigned to. It landed in e343534. The entry was inherited from an older draft and never re-checked against source, which is the exact failure that file’s own methodology note warns about.

makeDevResolveCtx has no such check, correctly: it trusts request headers by design, and index.ts refuses to boot in production without a real auth resolver unless ALLOW_DEV_AUTH=true is set deliberately.

The month grid was counted as unbuilt work

Listed as “the calendar is a card list, not a month grid”. MonthGrid exists and is a grid; what it deliberately does not do is lay slots over a real month calendar, for the reason its own comment states. Overstating a gap costs the same as understating one, so the entry has been narrowed to the filters, which are genuinely absent.

docs/GAPS.md was substantially out of date, and now is not

The previous pass flagged this as housekeeping. The file has been rewritten to contain only open items — 461 lines down to roughly 200 — with closed work deleted rather than ticked, and its own methodology note now says every claim was verified against the tree rather than carried forward. It and this page agree.

Three “frontend” gaps were not frontend gaps

Brand-knowledge upload, teams and roles, and the calendar’s account filter were all filed as frontend only — the tool is already there. Two of the three had only writes: knowledge.* could ingest a document and never answer “what does this brand know?”, and team.* could invite, promote and assign with no way to see whom. A settings panel built on those would have been close to useless — you cannot change somebody’s role without first knowing whose. The third could not filter by account because campaignSlots never selected the column. The classification was right about where the work was and wrong about whether a screen was sufficient.

Four doc comments claimed the opposite of what is now true

Each was accurate when written and became a false claim as the tools underneath it landed: onboarding said its connect and avatar steps had “no tool behind them yet”; AccountHome said the credit ledger had no read tool; settings/page.tsx described itself as holding the org half; team.ts said nothing enforced brand isolation at the query layer. All four fixed in the commits that falsified them — the last one had already been cited on this page as evidence of an open security hole.

npm run migrate could not find a connection string

Migrations 0029–0031 sat unapplied because the command failed with nothing but its driver choice printed. drizzle.config.ts read process.env.DATABASE_URL and nothing bridged apps/api/.env, so a fresh shell fell through to a placeholder host — turning “you have no connection string” into a connection failure against a host that does not exist. Fixed at the config, which every entry point shares, and the fallback now throws with the two places the value can come from. Thirty-two migrations are applied and drizzle-kit generate reports no schema drift.

The five cases a screenshot settles and a type system cannot

before the alpha

PerformancePanel with no campaign. Should be em dashes with reasons attached, never a column of confident zeros — nine of the fourteen metrics are nullable and that behaviour is the whole design of the component.

StallNotice. Needs a genuinely blocked item to render against. Cheapest way to make one: schedule a post to a platform with no connection and let the scheduler reach its five-attempt ceiling.

KnowledgePanel with nothing attached. The warning that every specific claim will be held is the most important state on that screen, and it is the state a brand is actually in on day one.

The brand-kit preview. A light background against the default white type is exactly the unreadable pair resolveKit deliberately refuses to guess around — the preview exists to make that visible before it reaches a render.

ConnectAccountsStep. The popup path, and the refusal-by-name for a platform with no configured developer app — which is most of them in this environment, and therefore the path most people will see.

ConversationDrawer on a keyless message. Rows written before threading existed report as their own conversation; the drawer should say so rather than render a one-turn transcript nobody can tell from a real one.

VariantResult in each of its three undecided states. The whole design is that "waiting to publish", "not enough reach" and "too close" read as different situations calling for different actions.
