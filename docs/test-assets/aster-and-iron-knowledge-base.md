## What we are

A jewellery and accessories workshop in Leith, Edinburgh. We make silver and
brass pieces by hand in small batches, take commissions, and remodel inherited
jewellery into something the owner will actually wear.

**Opened** October 2017, by Nadia Aster.
**Staff** 3 — two benchworkers, one who runs the shopfront and the commissions book.
**Studio** 14 Tolbooth Lane, open Wed–Sat 10am–5pm. Bench visible from the shop floor.

## What we sell

- **Ready-to-wear** — earrings, signet and stacking rings, cuffs, fine chain.
  £45–£320.
- **Commissions** — from £180. Four to six weeks. Two fittings included.
- **Remodels** — inherited or broken pieces reworked. Quoted after a bench
  appointment; we say no if the original is worth more intact.
- **Repairs** — resizing, restringing, stone resetting. £25–£90.
- **Accessories** — brass keyrings, collar pins, hair slides. £18–£60.

## How we talk

Plain, specific, unhurried. We name the metal, the weight and the maker on every
piece, because that is the difference between what we do and what a chain shop
does.

We assume the reader is buying something they intend to keep. That means we talk
about wear, repair and resizing as normal parts of owning a piece, not as
failures.

We do not use urgency. Nothing here is running out.

---

## Things we do not say

- Never "investment piece" or "will only go up in value". We are not selling an
  asset and we cannot know that.
- Never "ethically sourced" as a bare phrase — say what the source actually is:
  recycled sterling from a UK refiner, brass from Caledon Metals.
- Never "conflict-free" about anything other than stones we can document. Most of
  our stones are customer-supplied and we do not know their history.
- Never "handmade" about the chain, which we buy in. Say "hand-finished" where
  that is what happened.
- Never "one of a kind" about a batch piece. Say the batch size.
- Never "luxury". We are a workshop with a shopfront.
- No urgency language: "last chance", "selling fast", "limited time".
